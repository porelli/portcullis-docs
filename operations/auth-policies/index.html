Authentication Policies¶

Portcullis lets you require either or both of two hardware-backed factors (a passkey, a client certificate) for each app, with AND or OR semantics, plus a global default. Each app enforces only what its auth method can (see What each app can enforce). This page explains the model and how to use it.

Factors¶

Factor How the user proves it Keycloak executor amr value LoA
WebAuthn Taps a hardware security key (YubiKey, platform authenticator) webauthn-authenticator-passwordless hwk 2
mTLS Presents a client certificate over TLS (PIV / YubiKey cert) auth-x509-client-username-form mtls 3

LoA = Level of Authentication, a number Keycloak uses for step-up. Every sign-in is a passkey or a client cert and reaches LoA 2 in one touch (oauth2-proxy asks for hwk by default); LoA 3 needs the cert. First passkeys come from one-time setup links (./portcullis invite, the Hub's Users & Groups).

There is no password factor

The realm's browser flow has no password: its LoA 1 (pwd) subflow is a client cert or a passkey, the same as LoA 2. The Hub never offers or writes password, and its API refuses it (400). A stored policy that still names it is normalised when the Hub loads state.yaml, never into something weaker: in an all policy it is redundant and dropped; as an alternative (any, or alone) it accepted every session, so it becomes any of [mtls, webauthn]. The app's settings (or the Settings tab) show a note until the policy is saved. group-gate still parses password (met by every hwk/mtls session) but the Hub no longer publishes it.

Policy model¶

A policy has two parts:

  1. Factors — zero or more of mtls, webauthn.
  2. Op — any (any selected factor satisfies the policy) or all (every selected factor must have fired in the current session).

Special cases:

  • Zero factors → public (no authentication required).
  • One factor → the single factor is required; op is ignored.

Examples:

Policy Meaning
{factors: [], op: ""} Public
{factors: ["webauthn"]} A passkey
{factors: ["mtls", "webauthn"], op: "any"} Either mTLS or WebAuthn
{factors: ["mtls", "webauthn"], op: "all"} mTLS and WebAuthn (both must fire)

Configuring policies¶

In the Hub UI¶

Per-app — open an app in the catalog and use the Access policy block above the settings form:

  • Every app starts with Inherit global default ticked. While it inherits, the factor boxes are disabled and show the app's effective policy: the global default intersected with what the app can enforce, with a note when the two differ (e.g. "The global default allows mTLS or WebAuthn; this app can't enforce WebAuthn, so it enforces mTLS").
  • Untick Inherit to set an override. Only factors the app can enforce are selectable; the others are greyed out ("this app can't enforce it"), and the Hub refuses them with a 400 too.
  • If two or more are ticked, pick Require all checked (AND) or Any of checked (OR).
  • Apps that handle their own sign-in (custom, public, or an app switched to its own login only) show the block disabled: "This app handles its own sign-in".

Global default — the Settings tab exposes the default policy that every app without an override uses. Changing it affects all apps whose per-app policy is unset.

Fresh installs require a YubiKey

Until an admin saves a global default, the Hub uses any of [mtls, webauthn] ({"factors": ["mtls", "webauthn"], "op": "any"}). Every app without its own policy then requires the YubiKey, by either method: its client certificate (mtls, LoA 3) or a passkey (webauthn, LoA 2).

  • Routers get authaware@file: the certificate is optional at the handshake.
  • Keycloak steps up to at least LoA 2 (hwk).
  • group-gate accepts mtls or hwk in amr, so a certificate sign-in passes too (including enroll.<domain>/self renewal).
  • A password-only session gets the step-up page.

A WebAuthn-only default would send certificate users to the step-up page on every app. An explicitly saved public default is stored and honoured. It is not the same as "never set".

The safety score next to each policy is a cosmetic sum: 0 for public, LoA for a single factor, LoA + 0.5 per extra required factor for AND policies.

What each app can enforce¶

Auth method Factors Where
forward-auth, header-auth, custom behind Portcullis sign-in mtls, webauthn group-gate, on every request; a certificate is bound to the user (mTLS binding)
native-oidc mtls, webauthn Keycloak, when the user signs in to the app (acr of its client). mTLS is checked per connection only when the policy selects the protected@file tier (mTLS alone, or all including it)
mtls mtls The TLS handshake (protected@file), always: the certificate stays required even under a public or WebAuthn-only default, and an override can only be [mtls]
custom, public, own login only none The app handles its own sign-in; no policy, tier public@file

The effective policy is the configured one (override or global default) with the unsupported factors left out. For any that removes an alternative; for all it removes a requirement the app can't check, which the app's settings say. If nothing of the configured policy is enforceable (a WebAuthn-only default on an mTLS-only app), the app's own floor applies (the certificate) and the settings say so. The TLS tier and the Keycloak acr come from the effective policy. The policy file is unchanged: overrides are published as stored (they only name supported factors), inheriting apps publish nothing.

Apps with their own login¶

A custom template whose app has its own login and no SSO declares auth.native_login. Its settings then have a Sign-in choice:

  • Portcullis sign-in, then the app's own login (default). The Hub's override puts portcullis-auth@docker,portcullis-groups@docker in front of every router's own middlewares; group-gate requires the template's access_group and the policy applies per request, as for forward-auth.
  • App's own login only (no Portcullis), only if the template sets allow_without_gateway: true. The routers keep the template's middlewares without the gateway pair, the tier is public@file and no group is published: anyone on the internet reaches the app's login page. The UI shows a red warning, the switch is audited (app.sign_in) and the Hub logs it.

The template is never edited; both modes are compose-override labels, which the drift check compares with the running containers. If a template stops allowing the own-login-only mode, the Hub falls back to Portcullis sign-in.

What happens when a user hits an app¶

Same-LoA SSO (no step-up)¶

User signs in to Grafana with a passkey (policy: any of [mtls, webauthn], session acr=hwk). Opens Immich (policy: webauthn). The Keycloak session already satisfies the required LoA, so Immich gets a token with no further prompt. Same for any other apps with policies their session already satisfies.

Step-up on a stricter app¶

User signed in with a passkey (amr=[hwk], LoA 2). Opens Jellyfin with policy mtls-required. Keycloak sees the session's current LoA is 2, but Jellyfin's client requested acr_values=mtls (LoA 3). The Condition - Level of Authentication executor in the universal flow fires only the mTLS subflow — the certificate is asked for, nothing else. On success the session's LoA is upgraded to 3 and amr becomes [hwk, mtls]. No full re-login.

Step-up on forward-auth apps¶

oauth2-proxy ≥ 7.10 accepts per-request acr_values via the loginURLParameters alpha-config setting, which is what Portcullis runs (allowed values pwd, hwk, mtls; default hwk; kc_idp_hint=portcullis-invite and prompt=login exist only for setup links). When group-gate detects a session that doesn't satisfy the app's policy, it returns a 403 "Additional authentication required" page with the URL in an X-Stepup-URL header: https://auth.<domain>/oauth2/start?acr_values=<needed>&rd=https://<app>/. The step-up always goes through auth.<domain>; there is no /oauth2/ router on app hosts. There is no sign-out and no prompt=login: Keycloak's cookie authenticator runs the flow when the requested LoA is above the session's and asks only for the missing level. The new token reflects the step-up. When the missing factor is a hardware one (passkey / cert) the page does not auto-redirect: a Continue button, and links to register a passkey in the account console or enrol a certificate at enroll.<domain>. Other step-ups redirect after 2 seconds.

Public policy¶

A policy with no factors means group-gate requires no particular factor, and the Hub sets the router's tier to public@file. The Hub only changes tls.options. The template's middlewares stay as they are, so a forward-auth app still requires a Keycloak login and its group. An app is only reachable without logging in if its routers have no forward-auth middleware (auth.method: public, a custom app, or one switched to its own login only). An mtls app never becomes public: it keeps requiring the certificate.

Traefik TLS tier¶

The effective policy also drives the TLS tier on each router via a Hub-generated compose override:

Policy Tier Behaviour
Public, or the app handles its own sign-in public@file No client cert
mTLS-only, or all of [..., mtls] protected@file Requires client cert at TLS handshake
Everything else authaware@file Client cert tolerated but not required

The tier change is emitted as a per-router traefik.http.routers.<name>.tls.options label in ${HUB_DATA_DIR}/overrides/<app>.override.yml, for every router of the app. Base compose.yml files in templates are untouched. Changing the global default restarts only the inheriting apps whose tier actually changed.

One tier per host. Traefik selects TLS options per SNI host, before it knows the path, so every router of a host must share one tier. If two routers of a host disagree, Traefik falls back to its default TLS options and answers 421 Misdirected Request. The Hub therefore always writes the same tier for all of an app's routers, including its public router.

Public exceptions¶

Templates can declare share-link paths in auth.public_access; an admin turns them on per app in the Hub. While on, the Hub adds a router portcullis-public-<app> for those paths only:

  • higher priority than every router of the app, so it wins for its paths;
  • middlewares portcullis-public-strip-<app>@docker,rate-limit@file,security-headers@file only: no forward-auth, no group-gate, so no login, no group, no hardware key, no step-up and no access policy apply on these paths;
  • the strip middleware deletes client-supplied identity headers (core's X-Forwarded-User, X-Forwarded-Email, X-Forwarded-*, X-Auth-Request-*, Remote-*, plus the template's identity_headers, each also in its _ form), so a header-auth app can't be told who the visitor is;
  • its first middleware, portcullis-public-guard@docker (group-gate /public-guard), re-checks the path against the published patterns and answers 403 to requests carrying Authorization, Proxy-Authorization or the template's credential headers, cookies or query parameters (names normalised the way PHP does), so a stolen password, API key or session can't get past the hardware key there;
  • supporting paths serve static files only (by extension), plus single exact routes the template justifies;
  • in mode listed, only the share tokens an admin listed are routed; any other token on the same prefix still goes through forward-auth.

Exceptions vs mTLS. A policy of mtls alone, or all including mtls, normally gives the host protected@file: no client cert, no TLS handshake. Anonymous visitors can't present one, so while the public router exists the Hub uses authaware@file for all of the app's routers instead (policy.HostTier). What still enforces mTLS:

App type Gated paths after the downgrade
forward-auth / header-auth group-gate's mTLS binding requires a valid, unrevoked client certificate matching the user on every request, so mTLS stays enforced per request
native-oidc (no group-gate on the host-wide router) mTLS is only enforced at login, through the Keycloak acr (mtls). An existing app session works from a browser without the key
any router without group-gate loses the handshake check; the Hub lists such routers in the warning

In mode listed with no listed share there is no public router and no downgrade. Policies that already give authaware@file or public@file are unchanged.

Where policies are published¶

The Hub writes per-app policies into group-gate-policy.json keyed by every subdomain the app routes (never by template ID); apps that inherit the default publish nothing and group-gate uses the file's default. A policy with an unknown factor or op makes group-gate deny the request (fail closed). Format: Integration Contracts §4.

For native-OIDC apps the policy also sets the Keycloak client's default.acr.values, so Keycloak prompts for the right level at login.

Identity¶

Policies decide how a user signed in; who the user is always comes from the immutable LLDAP username (preferred_username, X-Forwarded-User), never from the email. LLDAP users can edit their own email, so Keycloak does not trust it (trustEmail=false) and nothing in Portcullis authorises or links accounts by it. See Security & Hardening.

mTLS binding¶

amr=mtls in a token only proves a certificate was used at login. When a policy is satisfied only through mtls (mtls alone, all including mtls, or any where mtls is the only factor in the token), group-gate additionally requires the current request to present an unrevoked client certificate whose CN equals the token's preferred_username. Otherwise the user gets the step-up page. A session opened with a since-revoked certificate, or replayed from a browser without the key, stops working at once. See TLS & mTLS.

Enforcement map¶

Layer Public WebAuthn mTLS AND combinations OR combinations
Traefik public authaware protected protected if mtls in set authaware unless mTLS-only
Keycloak none LoA 2 flow LoA 3 flow highest LoA + step-up lowest LoA (any satisfies)
group-gate no factor check require hwk ∈ amr require mtls ∈ amr + mTLS binding require all factors in amr (+ binding if mtls) require amr ∩ factors ≠ ∅ (+ binding if only mtls matched)

The table is per factor of the effective policy; which layers apply depends on the auth method (above).

LLDAP passwords¶

The browser flow never asks for an LLDAP password. LLDAP passwords remain for LLDAP's own web UI and for apps that bind to LDAP themselves. They are no policy factor.

Caveats¶

  • acr_values is advisory at Keycloak. The OIDC spec treats it as a hint; Keycloak only hard-rejects when a client requests via the claims parameter with essential: true. Portcullis uses the LoA conditional executors instead, which hard-fail when the user can't satisfy the level (e.g. asking for mtls without a certificate). group-gate also verifies acr/amr from the issued token as a belt-and-braces check — trust doesn't depend on Keycloak honoring the request.
  • Session LoA accumulates. Once a user steps up to LoA 3 via mTLS, every subsequent app within the same session sees acr=mtls and amr ⊇ [mtls, ...]. Stricter apps pass without additional prompts. Loosening policies mid-session doesn't downgrade the session.
  • max_age in the LoA conditions is 10 hours (matching the session lifespan). Lower this in realm-export.json if you want the re-prompt window to be shorter.

Troubleshooting¶

"Step up required" page from group-gate Your session is missing the factor(s) the app requires. Click the link in the page body to re-authenticate at the right level.

Client cert prompt loops indefinitely Your browser may not be sending the client certificate. Check that the cert is imported into your OS/keychain and that the origin matches the Traefik wildcard (*.<your-domain>).

Keycloak login form appears when you expected SSO The session cookie expired (8 hours idle / 12 hours max by default). Re-authenticate.

AND policy keeps failing even after a WebAuthn step-up Check that the acr client scope is attached to the app's Keycloak client and that the acr loa level protocol mapper has Add to access token = true. ./portcullis keycloak configure does this for portcullis-forward-auth, and the Hub attaches the acr and groups scopes to every client it registers; a manually-created client may be missing them.
